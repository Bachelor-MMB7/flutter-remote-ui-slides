## 1. Reddit-Antwort zur Dissertation Defense

You don't fit everything into it. You shouldn't. You should only include what the
audience could understand. Nevertheless, I'd recommend you make sure to include
the following:

- State your greater goal/objective and purpose (which you did not meet
  completely because ain't nobody got time for that)
- Summarize your approach to approach your greater goal
- List your n specific research question/objectives
- Explain RQ/objective #1, summarize your approach to tackle it, list
  assumptions, and state the (partial) answer you have for it
- Explain RQ/objective #2, summarize...
- ...
- Explain RQ/objective #n, summarize...
- Recap (partial) answers to your n RQs/objectives
- Acknowledge all limitations and everything you could not answer.
- Provide ideas for future work that can help bridge the gap you left toward your
  greater goal/objective purpose.

For your dissertation defense, you do not need to fit all your research into the
presentation. Your chair and your committee should have read your disseration and
therefore should know the details of it. Your audience does not need or want the
tedious details.

That said, both your committee and audience want a compelling narrative based on
the effective and efficient use of research data. The most important parts of that
narrative are your findings and your conclusions. Quickly summarize the context
that gives meaning to your findings and conclusions.

As a scholar, you should be able to introduce and contextualize your research in
10 minutes or less. Again, neither your committee nor your audience needs a
lenghty introduction and context.

Best of luck to you!

### Ergänzung aus demselben Text

To create a compelling thesis defense presentation, focus on visual clarity,
conciseness, and framing your research as a story, while also preparing thoroughly
for questions and maintaining a confident yet humble demeanor.

**Craft Your Presentation Visually and Concisely**

- Prioritize visuals over text. "If there is a slide with white background and
  black text with no color whatsoever I will personally shoot you."
- Break down complex information. "Labels and short concise phrases."
- Tell a story. "Weave it into the context of a story."

**Prepare for the Q&A Session**

- Anticipate questions. "Make a list of questions or topics you are scared will be
  asked and then come up with answers for those."
- Prepare "bonus" slides. "Make a few 'bonus' slides that you can use based around
  methodology, results, and discussion topics that didn't make the final cut in
  your thesis/paper."
- Address limitations proactively. "Have a clear cut limitations slide."

**Project Confidence and Professionalism**

- Practice extensively. "Practice, but take notes while you do the first couple
  times."
- Maintain composure. "Don't interrupt the committee asking questions."
- Be the expert. "You are expected to be the world's leading expert on your
  specific study."

**Understand the Committee's Role**

- They want you to succeed. "They're probably not going to try to 'get' you or trip
  you up. At this stage, they're generally rooting for you to succeed."
- They look for strong arguments. "Their job is to find the places where your
  argument could be stronger and push you on them."
- Admit what you don't know. "During Q&A, don't try to bluff."

## 2. Blogartikel: Thesis Defense Presentation — How To Structure the Slides and What To Cut

*18. März 2026, 9 min read. Content-Marketing für PapersFlow.ai.*

The hardest part of a thesis defense presentation is deciding what not to include.
The thesis may be 100 pages. The defense talk may be 20 minutes. That gap forces
ruthless selection.

**What the Committee Actually Needs**

Your committee usually needs to understand: the problem, the question, the method,
the key findings, the contribution, the limitations. They do not need every chapter
reproduced slide by slide.

**Recommended Defense Structure**

1. Title and framing
2. Problem and motivation
3. Research question
4. Method overview
5. Main finding 1
6. Main finding 2
7. Contribution
8. Limitations and future work
9. Conclusion

That is a defense structure, not a thesis table of contents.

**What To Cut**

Long literature review detail, every side analysis, every appendix result, every
background definition your committee already knows. Keep the slides focused on the
evaluative core of the thesis.

**Bottom Line**

A thesis defense presentation should make the committee's job easier: clear problem,
clear method, clear findings, clear contribution. If the deck feels like a compressed
thesis chapter dump, it is doing too much.

## 3. Blogartikel: Research Presentation Template

*18. März 2026, 8 min read. Content-Marketing für PapersFlow.ai.*

**Core Template**

```
1. Title
2. Problem / Motivation
3. Research Question or Objective
4. Method / Approach
5. Main Result
6. Secondary Support
7. Contribution / Implication
8. Limitations / Next Steps
9. Conclusion
10. References / Contact
```

This structure works for: conference talks, thesis defenses, research seminars,
paper presentations.

**Why This Template Works**

It keeps the logic visible: why the project matters, what was done, what was found,
why the finding matters. That is the argument most audiences need.

**Bottom Line**

A research presentation template should help you present the work clearly, not tempt
you into turning the deck into a paper substitute.
